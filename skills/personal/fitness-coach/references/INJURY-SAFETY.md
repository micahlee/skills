# Injury and Rehabilitation Safety

## Case Model

Timestamp every symptom report. Promote it to an active case when clinician-diagnosed, persistent/recurrent, function-limiting, test/rehab-triggering, or plan-changing.

Record:

- symptom location, quality, onset, behavior, severity, irritability, function, and trajectory;
- diagnostic status and confidence;
- clinician directions verbatim with source/date;
- questions, tests, exact method, result, and stop criteria;
- competing hypotheses and uncertainty;
- current modifications/rehab dose and during/immediate/24-hour response;
- review history and optional provider-ready summary.

Initial known case: long-head biceps tendinopathy/tendinitis symptoms. Treat the label as a sourced hypothesis unless clinician-confirmed.

## Workflow

1. Screen for urgent red flags and contraindications.
2. If present, explain the concern clearly, stop affected activity, and recommend appropriate urgent evaluation once. Record Micah’s decision; repeat only after material change.
3. Otherwise ask only missing discriminating questions.
4. Guide one low-risk self-test at a time only if it can change programming.
5. Explain purpose, limits, setup, expected response, and stop conditions before the test.
6. Update ranked hypotheses without declaring a diagnosis.
7. Preserve unaffected training where safe; modify loading, ROM, exercise, modality, or dose around the case.
8. Track response and progress, hold, or regress using the approved rules.

Do not guide skilled palpation, forceful provocation, or materially risky tests. Do not pressure Micah toward healthcare for slow improvement. A provider summary is available on request.

## Pain Monitoring

Use a personalized traffic-light model from clinician guidance, baseline symptoms, pain during activity, immediate functional change, and 24-hour response. Never silently impose a universal numeric pain cutoff.

Stop/regress for sharp or escalating pain, new neurological symptoms, traumatic change, meaningful strength/function loss, altered mechanics that increase risk, or violation of clinician constraints. Stable mild symptoms may be acceptable only when the case-specific plan permits them.

Every therapeutic recommendation requires reputable evidence, applicability notes, contraindications, stop conditions, confidence, and review date. Clinician directions take precedence.
